### Processing evidence precedes semantic confidence

OCR quality, parser behavior, series selection, table reconstruction, and model
or specialist version affect every downstream claim. A source citation is not
enough when the cited bytes were transformed incorrectly. Processing records
therefore become part of claim provenance.

### Entity resolution is a claim dependency

A perfectly extracted fact attached to the wrong company, vendor, patient,
clinic, specimen, account, or time period is still wrong. Candidate identities
stay quarantined until their anchors support a scoped link. Relationship edges
retain effective time and evidence rather than becoming timeless joins.

### Reconciliation preserves disagreement

Repeated dependent statements do not create independent support. Operational
authority and epistemic reliability remain separate, allowing an executed
agreement or charted result to be respected while its applicability or
clinical foundation is challenged.

### Admission creates a different object kind

The derived subject model remains immutable and non-authoritative. Subject
Hermes may admit selected results into evidence, timeline, purpose-bound
current state, memory candidates, or follow-up obligations under existing
gates. Admission never mutates the model into authoritative truth.

## Emergent behavior

This cluster can build coherent subject views across text, transactions,
communications, structured data, and images while preserving the ability to
answer:

- which source supports this assertion;
- how the source was processed;
- why the identity match was accepted;
- which conflicts remain;
- what is inferred versus observed;
- who reviewed the interpretation;
- which purpose may use it.

## Tensions to hold

- High-fidelity provenance produces complex graphs that must still be usable
  by humans under time pressure.
- Specialist review improves assurance but can become the throughput and cost
  bottleneck.
- Purpose-specific projections reduce disclosure but can disagree or age at
  different rates.
